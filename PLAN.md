# Lesson plan — 19 lessons

The arc that gets a German speaker with no Japanese to the end of the four
classes. One grammar point per lesson, taught 3–5 times inside a dialogue,
22 lines, ending on homework the next lesson checks.

## The story

Jonas works in an office. A new colleague from Osaka, **中村** (Nakamura),
starts on his team. Aoki is his Japanese teacher. Every lesson opens with
something Jonas actually needs *that day* — never "I want to learn Japanese".
Nakamura arriving, then working alongside him, supplies those needs for the
whole arc without inventing a new premise each time.

Roles never swap: Aoki teaches and is always right, Jonas asks and sometimes
guesses wrong.

## Why this order differs from the four classes

The original Class 1 opens with `わたしは コーヒーを のみます`, which needs は
and を to already mean something — but は arrives in Class 2 and を in Class 3.
A textbook survives that because the explanation sits under the sentence. A
spoken dialogue cannot: there is no page to look down at.

So は comes first (it is written and passing already), then sentence shape,
then the object, then the rest. Class 4's four ideas are split: three of them
are one insight ("Japanese does not mark what German marks"), politeness is a
separate and much more consequential topic.

## The 19

### Class 1 — how a sentence is built

| # | Grammar | Jonas needs to… | New words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | は — the topic | introduce himself before Nakamura arrives tomorrow | 私, 学生, です, は |
| 2 | か — the question | ask Nakamura something on her first day | あなた, はい, いいえ, 大阪出身 |
| 3 | verb at the end | say what he *does*, not just what he is | のみます, たべます, verb-final order |
| 4 | を — the object | say **what** he drinks, not just that he drinks | を, コーヒー, お茶 |
| 5 | ます / ません | turn down coffee politely without sounding rude | ません |
| 6 | **recap** | check all four before Nakamura's first team lunch | — |

Lesson 3 is the one that has to land: the verb goes last, and German puts it
second. It is taught with a topic and a verb only — no object yet — so the
shape is visible with nothing else moving.

### Class 2 — what Japanese leaves unsaid

| # | Grammar | Jonas needs to… | New words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | dropping the topic | stop saying 私は in every sentence — Nakamura noticed | — |
| 8 | restating the topic | make clear he switched from himself to someone else | 中村さん, さん |
| 9 | **recap** | retell a short exchange, leaving out what is obvious | — |

Lesson 7 is the pair to lesson 1: first you learn to state the topic, then you
learn to stop. Lesson 8 is the guard rail — omission is not "never say it",
it is "say it when the listener would otherwise guess wrong".

### Class 3 — particles as role markers

| # | Grammar | Jonas needs to… | New words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | が — new information | answer "who did that?" about a mix-up at work | が, だれ |
| 11 | に — where to, when | agree a time and place to meet Nakamura | に, 行きます, 時 |
| 12 | で — where it happens | say where the team eats, not where they go | で, 食べます, 会社 |
| 13 | all five together | get through one real exchange using は, が, を, に, で | — |
| 14 | **recap** | say which particle marks which role, from memory | — |

Lesson 10 keeps が vs は deliberately light: が introduces what is new,
は talks about what is already known. No exhaustive rule — one contrast, one
situation, stated definitely but scoped to that sentence.

Lesson 13 writes no new grammar at all. It is the lesson where the five
particles stop being five separate facts.

### Class 4 — what Japanese does not have, and how polite to be

| # | Grammar | Jonas needs to… | New words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | no articles, no gender | stop looking for der/die/das and a/an | — |
| 16 | no plural marking | say "colleagues" without a plural ending | 人, 会社の人 |
| 17 | です / ます as default | know which register he has been speaking all along | — |
| 18 | casual, for recognition | understand Nakamura talking to a friend, without copying it | だ, casual endings |
| 19 | **final recap** | hold one short conversation using the whole arc | — |

Lesson 18 teaches recognition only, and says so plainly inside the lesson:
Jonas hears casual Japanese, understands it, and keeps speaking politely.
A beginner who starts mixing registers at work causes a problem that a
beginner who stays polite never has.

## What each lesson must satisfy

Every lesson, before it is pushed:

- 22 lines, `Sprecher: text`, Aoki and Jonas alternating, Aoki has the last line
- opens on a concrete need, closes on homework the next lesson checks
- one grammar point, appearing 3–5 times
- at least 10 tags; no tag repeated more than 3× except the target grammar
- every particle taught also appears inside at least one whole-sentence tag
- no bare romaji and no untagged Japanese in either language's prose
- understandable by ear alone — no "as you saw above"
- rules stated definitely but scoped to the sentence, never "it depends"
- `check.py` clean, then a model review, then the Portuguese translation, then
  the DE/PT pairing check

## Vocabulary load

Cumulative, roughly 4–6 new items per lesson, ~60 by lesson 19. Recaps add
none. That is deliberately small: the arc teaches how Japanese is *shaped*,
not a word list. Vocabulary can grow later on a structure the learner already
understands.

## Open questions

1. **Nakamura's name.** Introduced in lesson 8 as 中村さん. If a different name
   reads better, change it before lesson 3 — she is referred to from there on.
2. **Lesson 5 and lesson 17 both touch ます.** Lesson 5 is the mechanics
   (ます / ません). Lesson 17 is the register (why this is the default). If that
   feels like one lesson rather than two, 17 merges into 16 and the arc is 18.
3. **Lesson 13 writes no new grammar.** If consolidation lessons feel like a
   waste of a slot, it can be dropped for an extra particle lesson instead.
